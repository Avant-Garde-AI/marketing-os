# Building On-Taste Storyboards and Narratives for an AI Creative Agent: What's Known, What's Been Tried, and Where the Frontier Is

## TL;DR
- **Template/plan-first scaffolding is the single most validated architectural choice you can make**: across the LLM story-generation literature (Plan-and-Write, Re3, DOC, Dramatron), decomposing generation into plan → draft → revise measurably improves coherence, relevance, and even "interestingness" — DOC beat its own predecessor Re3 by 22.5% absolute on plot coherence and 20.7% on interestingness in human eval. Your "work backwards from a template" hypothesis is correct and well-supported, but it carries a documented cost: homogenization. Doshi & Hauser (Science Advances 2024) found generative-AI assistance makes stories "more creative, better written, and more enjoyable" individually while making them "more similar to each other than stories by humans alone" — "an increase in individual creativity at the risk of losing collective novelty." That is the exact enemy of "taste."
- **The real research frontier — and your leverage — is NOT the generation stack (largely commoditized by LTX Studio, Runway, StoryDiffusion, and multi-agent storyboard papers) but the taste/reward layer**: aesthetic and brand-taste alignment from small samples, reliable critique that doesn't regress to the mean, and tying creative structure to measured behavioral performance. This is precisely where NeuroGraph's functional grounding (personas, decision factors, performance signal) is a defensible moat that pure generation tools lack.
- **Concrete build recommendation**: adopt a hierarchical neuro-symbolic architecture — a symbolic "creative schema/beat-sheet" layer (archetypes, hook taxonomy, brand constraints) driving a plan→draft→critique→revise LLM harness, with taste encoded via curated exemplar retrieval + lightweight LoRA/style vectors rather than heavy fine-tuning, and a critic that is explicitly grounded in performance data rather than self-generated preference. Treat "taste as a curated dataset," not as a prompt.

## Key Findings

**1. Plan-first scaffolding works, and the evidence is unusually consistent.** The lineage runs from symbolic planners (TALE-SPIN, Meehan 1977; MEXICA, Pérez y Pérez & Sharples 2001) through neural plan-and-write (Yao et al., AAAI 2019) to modern LLM harnesses (Dramatron, Mirowski et al., CHI 2023; Re3, Yang et al., EMNLP 2022; DOC, Yang et al., ACL 2023). Every serious system converges on the same shape: build a structured plan/outline first, then generate constrained by it, then revise. The measured gains are real and repeatable.

**2. The homogenization tax is equally well-documented.** Doshi & Hauser (Science Advances 2024, vol. 10 no. 28, eadn5290) plus a cluster of 2024–2026 studies (Anderson, Shah & Kreminski, Creativity & Cognition 2024; a PNAS Nexus study on "homogeneously creative" LLMs) show LLM assistance raises individual quality while narrowing collective diversity. Structure amplifies this. For a taste-driven creative agent this is the central tension you must engineer against.

**3. Storyboarding as an explicit intermediate representation is now standard practice**, both in research (a wave of 2024–2026 multi-agent script→storyboard→video papers) and commercially (LTX Studio's "storyboard-first" workflow). The storyboard as a structured, editable artifact between script and render is validated — this is your natural sub-module boundary.

**4. Aesthetic alignment has mature tooling for image quality but not for "taste."** Reward models (LAION aesthetic predictor, ImageReward, HPS v2, PickScore) and preference tuning (Diffusion-DPO) reliably push toward generically "pleasing" images — but that's the problem: they regress toward a population mean and are prone to reward hacking and diversity collapse. Personal/brand taste is a different, under-solved problem best approached via few-shot personalization (DreamBooth/LoRA/textual inversion) and curation.

**5. LLM-as-critic is useful but unreliable for creative quality specifically.** Self-Refine and Reflexion work for verifiable tasks; for open-ended creative work, self-critique can amplify self-bias and drift toward style over substance. LitBench (Fein et al., arXiv:2507.00769, EACL 2026) shows the best off-the-shelf judge, Claude-3.7-Sonnet, reaches only 73% agreement with human preference (GPT-4.1/DeepSeek-R1 at 70–71%), while purpose-trained Bradley-Terry and generative reward models both reach 78%. TTCW (Chakrabarty et al., "Art or Artifice?", CHI 2024, arXiv:2309.14556) found "none of the LLMs positively correlate with the expert assessments."

**6. Creative attributes DO drive measured behavioral outcomes, and this is your strongest strategic asset.** The foundational academic result (Azimi et al., WWW 2012) showed visual features alone predict CTR "up to 3.27 times better than Weighted Sampling policy." Industry primary sources — NCSolutions & Nielsen "Five Keys to Advertising Effectiveness" (Aug 2023): "advertising creative drives nearly half (49%) of incremental sales and remains the most critical driver of advertising effectiveness by a wide margin" — establish creative quality as the dominant controllable driver of ad effectiveness. This is exactly the bridge NeuroGraph is positioned to own.

## Details

### 1. Computational narrative & story generation

**Classical heritage (still matters as structure, not as engine).** The symbolic era established the vocabulary you'll reuse. TALE-SPIN (Meehan, IJCAI 1977) generated stories via character goal-planning; MEXICA (Pérez y Pérez & Sharples 2001) introduced the engagement–reflection cycle and case-library-driven narrative built from emotional/tension arcs; partial-order planners (UCPOP) and later narrative planners (Riedl & Young; Ware & Siler's Sabre, AIIDE 2021, supporting intention and theory of mind) formalized plot as goal-directed action sequences. The enduring lesson from the neuro-symbolic survey literature (e.g., the METATRON framework, MDPI Mathematics 2025, which combines Polti's 36 dramatic situations as a symbolic planning taxonomy with fine-tuned LLMs for realization): **symbolic systems gave coherence and control but lacked novelty and fluency; neural systems gave fluency but lost control. The productive path is hybrid.** Polti's dramatic situations, Propp's morphology, and Campbell/Booker-style archetypes remain valuable as the *schema layer* — the "template" you want to elaborate — not as generators.

**LLM-era structured generation (the core of your harness design):**
- **Plan-and-Write (Yao et al., AAAI 2019, arXiv:1811.05701):** first plan a storyline (sequence of keywords/events), then realize it. Compared static vs. dynamic schema planning; found explicit planning yields stories "more diverse, coherent, and on topic" by both automatic and human eval. This is the canonical evidence that plan-first beats flat generation.
- **Dramatron (Mirowski et al., CHI 2023, arXiv:2209.14958):** hierarchical prompt-chaining with a 70B Chinchilla model generating title → characters → plot beats → location descriptions → dialogue from a single log line. Evaluated with 15 professional playwrights/screenwriters; several scripts were staged ("Plays by Bots"). Key qualitative findings directly relevant to you: writers praised hierarchical generation for letting them work at the arc level, but flagged **lack of subtext/nuance and logical gaps**, and noted the top-down structure "does not correspond to every writer's process." Co-writing tool, never autonomous.
- **Re3 (Yang, Tian, Peng & Klein, EMNLP 2022, arXiv:2210.06774):** the Plan–Draft–Rewrite–Edit framework. Generates 2,000–2,500-word stories by (a) prompting for a structured plan, (b) recursively reprompting with plan + story-state context, (c) reranking continuations for coherence/relevance, (d) editing for factual consistency. Human evaluators judged Re3 stories substantially more plot-coherent (+14% absolute) and premise-relevant (+20%) than same-length flat generation from the same base model.
- **DOC (Yang, Klein, Peng & Tian, ACL 2023, arXiv:2212.10077):** extends Re3 with a "detailed outliner" (expands a brief outline into a hierarchical one, shifting creative burden to planning) and a "detailed controller" (keeps drafting faithful to outline). Beat Re3 by **22.5% absolute on plot coherence, 28.2% on outline relevance, and 20.7% on interestingness** in human eval, and was judged much more controllable interactively. Crucially, the DOC paper explicitly documents the **control-strength tradeoff**: "too low strength risks deviating from the constraint, while too high strength risks narrowly-focused, repetitive generations." This is the constraint-vs-freedom dial you must expose.
- **Wordcraft (Yuan et al., IUI 2022; Writers Workshop, Ippolito et al. 2022):** Google's LLM story editor with custom user-defined controls. The pivotal finding for a creative tool: **writers valued suggestions "despite needing to substantially revise the text, or not using the text at all"** — success metric was "inspiring seed text," not perfect prose. And the workshop's blunt conclusion: "using LaMDA to write complete stories is a dead end. It is a much more effective tool when used to add spice."

**Why plan-first helps and where it fails.** It helps because it externalizes long-range structure (plot, character arc, causality) that flat autoregressive generation loses over long horizons — the coherence gains above are the evidence. It fails in three documented ways: (a) **homogenization/regression to the mean** (below); (b) **loss of surprise** — over-strong outline control produces repetitive, "safe" text (DOC's own finding); (c) **planning brittleness** — if the plan is generic, the story is generic (the "boring premise → boring story" problem noted in the controllability survey, arXiv:2102.12634).

**The homogenization problem (design against this).** Doshi & Hauser (Science Advances 2024) is the flagship result: access to generative-AI ideas "causes stories to be evaluated as more creative, better written, and more enjoyable... However, generative AI–enabled stories are more similar to each other than stories by humans alone," yielding "an increase in individual creativity at the risk of losing collective novelty." Anderson, Shah & Kreminski (Creativity & Cognition 2024, "Homogenization Effects of Large Language Models on Human Creative Ideation") found LLM-assisted ideation produces less semantically diverse ideas. A PNAS Nexus study ("Large language models are homogeneously creative") found outputs converge regardless of which model is used. A preregistered study on 2,200 college-admission essays found each additional human essay contributed more new ideas than each additional GPT-4 essay, and the gap widened with scale and "persisted despite efforts to enhance AI content through both prompt and parameter modifications." **Implication: diversity must be engineered in explicitly (diverse exemplar sampling, anchorless/parallel ideation, temperature/structure decoupling), not assumed.**

**Controllability of tone/pacing/beats/arc.** MEGATRON-CNTRL (controllable generation with external knowledge), reward-shaping approaches (Tambwekar et al., IJCAI 2019, controllable plot via reward shaping), and neuro-symbolic systems (WhatELSE, arXiv:2502.18641, "configurable level of abstraction"; StoReys, ICCC 2024; the PDDL-grounded neurosymbolic pipeline in AIIDE) all target exactly the beat/arc/tone controls you need. The recurring architecture: symbolic layer holds the controllable structure; LLM realizes it. TombWriter (arXiv:2605.19681) is a good recent template for beat-level human-AI control with Simulation/Nudging/Manual-edit modes.

### 2. Storyboarding specifically (text-to-sequence, visual narrative)

**Visual storytelling foundations.** The VIST dataset (Huang et al., 2016; 210,819 images, 10,117 Flickr albums, five-image/five-sentence stories) is the canonical benchmark but is short and simplistic; successors enrich it (VIST++, VWP/Visual Writing Prompts, SCO-VIST with social-commonsense graphs, RoViST for robust metrics). For movie/cinematic storyboards, **Storyboard20K** (shots sampled from MovieNet and LSMDC with shot-by-shot synopses) and VinaBench (2025, faithful/consistent visual narratives, built from VWP + Storyboard20K + StorySalon) are the more relevant benchmarks.

**Story visualization / consistent characters (the render layer — commoditizing fast).** The lineage: StoryGAN → StoryDALL-E → Make-A-Story → AutoStory (Wang et al., IJCV 2024, arXiv:2311.11243; LLM plans sparse layout/bounding boxes, diffusion renders dense — a clean division of labor: "LLMs are great at planning, diffusion models are better at rendering when guided by dense signals") → StoryDiffusion (Zhou et al., NeurIPS 2024; consistent self-attention injecting reference tokens across frames). Recent work adds agentic consistency repair: Audit & Repair (Akdemir et al., arXiv:2506.18900; a multi-agent audit-and-repair loop for cross-panel consistency, model-agnostic, works with Flux). Character consistency — historically the hardest problem — is now largely a solved-and-productized capability.

**Script→storyboard→video multi-agent pipelines (2024–2026, very active).** A striking convergence on the same architecture appears across MovieAgent (arXiv:2503.07314, multi-agent CoT planning: director → scene → shot decomposition), Camera Artist (arXiv:2604.09195, Director Agent + Cinematography Shot Agent with "Recursive Shots Generation" and a fine-tuned LLM for "Cinematic Language Injection"), CANVAS (arXiv:2604.13452, "Visual Agentic Storyboarding"), Dialogue Director (arXiv:2412.20725, Story Director → Cinematographer → Storyboard Maker), StoryBlender (arXiv:2604.03315, grounds the storyboard in a 3D scene with a continuity memory graph), and DramaDirector (arXiv:2606.24107, geometry-guided short-drama). **The shared pattern you should adopt: a Director/Story agent expands brief→structured scenes; a Cinematography agent decomposes scenes→shots with explicit cinematic parameters; a consistency/memory layer (a continuity graph) prevents drift.** Camera Artist's explicit finding is directly useful: general-purpose LLMs "produce generic prompts rather than leveraging professional cinematic language," which they fix with a fine-tuned cinematic-language model — evidence that domain fine-tuning on cinematic vocabulary is one place fine-tuning pays off.

**Cinematic language formalization.** These systems encode shot size, framing, camera angle, lighting type/condition, composition, and camera movement as explicit structured fields (see Camera Artist's CoT and VideoMemory's Storyboard Agent JSON schema: "what happens, where, who appears, which props"). The 180-degree rule, edit rhythm, and continuity are handled implicitly via memory graphs and shot-type sequencing (start/mid/end) rather than formal cinematographic grammars — a genuine gap you could exploit.

**Commercial tooling.** LTX Studio (Lightricks) is the clearest public example of the storyboard-first pattern: paste a script → auto-decompose into scenes → generate storyboard thumbnails with suggested framing → carry frames "frame for frame into video generation without re-keying a single character or prop." Their own framing: "treat the storyboard as the first generated artifact of the project." Elements system handles character/prop consistency; timeline editor for pacing. Runway, Luma, Krea, Higgsfield, Flora occupy adjacent territory. **What's public: the workflow and the storyboard-as-IR concept. What's proprietary: the taste/quality layer and any performance grounding — again, where your differentiation lives.**

### 3. Aesthetic and taste alignment

**Aesthetic/quality reward models (mature, generic).** The LAION aesthetic predictor (Schuhmann 2022; CLIP embeddings + MLP) is the baseline. ImageReward (Xu et al., NeurIPS 2023; 137K expert comparisons), HPS v2 (Wu et al., 2023; 798K annotations on 434K image pairs), and PickScore (Kirstain et al., NeurIPS 2023; trained on Pick-a-Pic's 500K+ real user preference pairs) are the workhorses. Diffusion-DPO (Wallace et al.; fine-tuned SDXL on 851K Pick-a-Pic pairs) and step-level variants (SPO) reliably improve "visual appeal" and prompt alignment. **Critical limitation for taste: these encode a population average of "pleasing."** The reward-model literature itself shows they correlate weakly with pure aesthetics (one analysis: HPSv2 aesthetics correlation ~0.007–0.191) and are dominated by prompt-alignment signal.

**Reward hacking and diversity collapse (the hard wall).** A consistent 2024–2026 finding: optimizing aesthetic/preference rewards collapses diversity. DiverseGRPO (arXiv:2512.21514) documents "homogenized results (nearly identical character appearances)" from GRPO-based preference optimization — "reward maximization tends to overfit the model to a narrow subset of high-reward modes... suppressing creative or unconventional outputs." Reward-hacking surveys catalog mode collapse, blank/blurry outputs, and artifact-prone generation. The "RLAIF doom loop" practitioner writeup warns that letting AI-generated preference data replace the human-anchored fraction poisons the fine-tune. **Implication: heavy preference-tuning toward any single reward is actively dangerous for a taste product; you want the mean pushed up but the distribution kept wide.**

**Small-sample taste alignment (your actual problem).** This is where the leverage is. Textual Inversion (Gal et al., ICLR 2022; learn a new token embedding), DreamBooth (Ruiz et al., CVPR 2023; fine-tune on a few subject images), Custom Diffusion, StyleDrop (style-specific), and LoRA-based PEFT are the toolkit for encoding a specific look from few examples. The documented tradeoffs matter: a 2026 study (arXiv:2510.09475, few-shot multi-token DreamBooth+LoRA) found plain DreamBooth "memorizes the single-subject training data" (overfits), Textual Inversion "lacks the capacity to simultaneously adapt to identity and style," and LoRA reduces training-copy tendency but can overfit to regularization data. **"Taste as a dataset" (curation-based alignment) — assembling and continuously curating a house-style exemplar corpus and retrieving from it — is the most robust, lowest-risk path, and it composes with light LoRA/style-vector encoding.**

**LLM/model-as-critic — useful but don't trust it to define quality.** Self-Refine (Madaan et al., NeurIPS 2023; generate→critique→revise, no training) improves many tasks, and multi-agent writer/critic loops are a reasonable engineering pattern. But documented failure modes are exactly the ones that hurt creative work: **self-bias amplification** (Xu et al., ACL 2024, "Pride and Prejudice: LLM Amplifies Self-Bias in Self-Refinement"), **feedback drift toward style over substance, and over-editing that removes good content.** On evaluation reliability: LitBench (Fein et al., EACL 2026, arXiv:2507.00769; 43,827 training + 2,480 test story pairs from Reddit) found the best off-the-shelf judge (Claude-3.7-Sonnet) only 73% agreement with human preference (GPT-4.1/DeepSeek-R1 at 70–71%; adding chain-of-thought actually dropped the generative reward model to 72%), while purpose-trained Bradley-Terry and generative reward models both reached 78%. TTCW (Chakrabarty et al., "Art or Artifice?", CHI 2024, arXiv:2309.14556; 48 stories, 2,000+ expert tests) found LLM-generated stories "pass 3-10X less TTCW tests than stories written by professionals" and "none of the LLMs positively correlate with the expert assessments" — though human experts themselves only reached moderate agreement (Fleiss κ 0.41). HANNA (Chhun et al.; 1,056 stories, 19,008 annotations across six criteria) confirmed BLEU/ROUGE are "remarkably poor" at story-level quality. **Takeaway: a critic is worth having, but it should be (a) purpose-trained on your domain and ideally on performance-labeled data, not an off-the-shelf LLM judge, and (b) treated as a filter/ranker, not the definition of taste.**

### 4. Template / archetype / schema representations

**Reusable creative schemas.** The neuro-symbolic tradition gives you concrete precedents for representing structure as reusable schemas populated by neural generation: METATRON (Polti's 36 dramatic situations → Attribute-Value Matrices populated from a knowledge base of archetypes/settings → LLM realization), the "Garden of Forking Paths" narrative-arc-conditioned planner (arXiv:2605.01245, generate-first-constrain-later, arc states forming archetypes like Cinderella/Icarus), and PDDL-grounded neurosymbolic pipelines (AIIDE). The pattern: **a symbolic scaffold (archetype + roles + beats + constraints) is instantiated with controlled randomness for diversity, then realized and coherence-filtered by an LLM.**

**Ad-creative taxonomies tied to performance (directly applicable to your schema layer).** Performance-marketing practice has developed exactly the "creative DNA" tagging you describe. VidMob's Aperture "dissects ads into tens of thousands of measurable elements across categories like storytelling and tone and connects them with performance" (company reports trillions of creative tags linked to impressions; a VidMob × MMA Global × Kellanova study analyzed 443 creative assets across Meta). Alison.ai and computer-vision tagging layers auto-tag format/objects/colors/text. Orlando Wood's System1 work (*Lemon*, IPA 2019) provides an empirically validated feature taxonomy: "right-brain" features (characters, whole scenes, narrative, humor, sense of place, melodic music) correlate with higher effectiveness Star ratings; "left-brain" features (flat, product-in-isolation) correlate with lower — from a test of 100 UK + 100 US ads drawn at random from System1's Ad Ratings database. System1's "fluent devices" analysis (300+ IPA-databank campaigns dating to 1992) found recurring-character/scenario campaigns "significantly more likely to drive profit and market share gain" and to reduce price sensitivity. **These are ready-made, performance-validated schema vocabularies you can encode directly as your beat/hook/archetype taxonomy.**

**How much structure is optimal.** The DOC control-strength finding, the symbolic-vs-neural tradeoff (control vs. novelty), and the homogenization literature all point to the same conclusion: **structure should constrain the strategically-important dimensions (brand safety, on-strategy messaging, persona fit, hook type) tightly, while deliberately leaving the aesthetically/emotionally-important dimensions (specific imagery, phrasing, surprise) loose.** There is no published universal optimum; the actionable principle is per-dimension control granularity, not a single global "creativity slider."

### 5. Fine-tuning vs. harness vs. in-context

**The comparative evidence.** For on-taste creative generation the practitioner and research consensus is nuanced:
- **In-context / exemplar retrieval** is favored for speed, iteration, and when data is scarce — the dominant recommendation for brand-voice work ("prompt with brand guidelines + 3–5 reference examples; do not fine-tune; in-context examples are faster to iterate"). But it suffers example-choice bias, order sensitivity, and context-length cost.
- **Fine-tuning** wins when you have a stable house style and enough exemplars, and when prompt-maintenance becomes unwieldy. A 2025 paper (arXiv:2507.04889, "Fine-tuning on simulated data outperforms prompting for agent tone of voice") found fine-tuning beat prompting for consistent voice. Practitioner reports cite fine-tuning reducing "voice drift by 40–60%." An e-commerce marketing-copy system (arXiv:2505.23809) used LoRA fine-tuning on brand-tagged examples plus a rule-based review module (sensitive-word filters, brand-compliance checks, human review).
- **Agentic multi-role harness (writer / art director / critic)** is the emerging default for complex creative pipelines (all the storyboard multi-agent papers), and composes with either of the above.
- **Hybrid is the practical winner**: fine-tune/LoRA for the stable stylistic base, in-context exemplars for campaign-specific steering, symbolic schema for strategy/constraints, and a filtering/critic layer for quality control.

**Data requirements and cold-start.** Few-shot personalization (DreamBooth/LoRA) needs only a handful of images per concept/style; brand-voice few-shot classification runs on 5–10 exemplars, while fine-tuning a discriminator wants 100–1,000 on-brand/off-brand pairs. Synthetic data generation (as in the tone-of-voice paper) is a validated cold-start mitigation. The realistic caution (from a predictive-creative-scoring practitioner writeup): "a model trained on 200 assets from one vertical will not generalize well to a different product category or platform" — so per-brand/per-vertical adaptation, not one universal taste model.

**What actually works in production (synthesis):** curated exemplar corpus + retrieval + light PEFT for style + explicit symbolic constraints for brand/strategy + automated filtering + a human go/no-go gate. Every credible production writeup converges on "automate the detection, not the final decision."

### 6. Evaluation

**How the field measures "good."** Human evaluation on multi-dimensional rubrics (coherence, relevance, interestingness, empathy, surprise, complexity) remains the gold standard — Re3/DOC use pairwise human coherence/interestingness judgments; HANNA defines six social-science criteria; the Psychological Depth Scale (2024) reports strong human inter-rater agreement (Krippendorff's α 0.72) but LLM-human correlation averaging only 0.51. Automated metrics (BLEU/ROUGE/CIDEr, still used on VIST) are widely shown inadequate for creative quality. Purpose-trained reward models (LitBench) beat off-the-shelf judges but cap around 78% human agreement. **Practical protocol for you: pairwise human preference on a fixed rubric for ground truth; a purpose-trained reward model as a scalable proxy; never rely on generic automated metrics or an off-the-shelf LLM judge alone.**

**Tying creative quality to behavioral outcomes (your differentiator — strong evidence).**
- **Foundational academic result:** Azimi et al. ("The Impact of Visual Appearance on User Response in Online Display Advertising," WWW 2012; Yahoo! Labs) extracted 43 visual features from real RightMedia creatives (datasets of 6,272 and 3,888 creatives, each ≥100K impressions) and predicted CTR "up to 3.27 times better than Weighted Sampling policy... from visual features only," with concrete design rules (higher contrast, few salient centered/rule-of-thirds components, color harmony → higher CTR; clutter, too many characters, too many hues → lower CTR). Follow-ons: Cheng et al. (KDD 2012, multimedia features for click prediction), Zhao et al. (CIKM 2019, creative cold-start), Chen et al. AutoCO (WWW 2021, Alibaba automated creative optimization; "aesthetic creatives may improve CTR").
- **Creative is the dominant controllable driver of ad performance.** NCSolutions & Nielsen "Five Keys to Advertising Effectiveness" (Aug 2023; meta-study of nearly 450 CPG campaigns modeling 18 features via ML): **"advertising creative drives nearly half (49%) of incremental sales and remains the most critical driver of advertising effectiveness by a wide margin"** (brand 21%, reach 14%, targeting 11%, recency 5%). Nielsen ("Want a Successful Ad? Get Creative," 2017): creative quality "contributes as much to a brand's in-market success as all other factors combined," up to 80% (TV) / 89% (digital) when creative is strong. Google/Nielsen: "effective creative accounts for almost 50% of ROI"; ads following Google's ABCD principles saw "30% higher sales lift." Meta/Facebook IQ cites "creativity drives 56% of a campaign's sales ROI." Critically, a **marketer-perception gap** exists: an Advertiser Perceptions survey (Feb 2024, 122 marketers) found marketers estimate creative at only ~19% of total sales effect — about 2.5× *below* the 49% reality. The upshot: buyers systematically undervalue exactly the thing Atelier + NeuroGraph would optimize.
- **Attention-based measurement (the mechanism linking creative to memory/sales).** Karen Nelson-Field / Amplified Intelligence established the "attention memory threshold": memory retention begins around ~2.5 seconds of active attention, with each additional active-attention second worth ~3 days in memory, and a "strong and significant relationship between active attention seconds and Short Term Advertising Strength (STAS)." A 2025 VCCP Media × Amplified study (20,000+ views of 72 digital video ads, original vs. brand-asset-stripped "bad twins") found distinctive brand assets can cut the threshold to ~1.5 seconds. Dentsu's "The Attention Economy" (2021, MRS Award winner) found "attention is three times better at predicting outcomes than viewability," and Dentsu's "The Brand Reset" (2025, with Kantar & Lumen) quantified a single ad exposure driving "on average, a 1%–5% increase in sales... over the following three years."
- **Long/short and emotional structure (Binet & Field, IPA, *The Long and the Short of It*, 2013; 996 campaigns, 1980–2010):** the ~60:40 brand-building:activation optimum; emotional campaigns "almost twice as likely" to drive top-box profit growth; fame-driving campaigns ~4× as efficient at market-share growth per 10 pts of excess SOV. Paul Dyson (Admap 2014): creative execution quality is the 2nd-largest profitability driver, multiplying profitability ~12×; highly-awarded creative up to 16× as efficient (Binet & Field, "Selling Creativity Short").

*(Source-quality flag: the academic CTR papers and NCSolutions/Binet-Field are the strongest primary sources. The Nelson-Field "2.5s / 3 days" figures and vendor studies (Dentsu, VidMob, System1, Google/Meta self-reported) are credible industry research but largely conference/commissioned rather than peer-reviewed — cite as industry evidence, not settled science. Higher "creative = 70%" vendor figures trace back to the more robust ~49–56% primary numbers.)*

### 7. Frontier + gaps (2024–2026)

**Genuinely unsolved / active frontier:**
1. **Small-sample brand-taste alignment that preserves diversity** — encoding a specific aesthetic from few exemplars without collapsing to a mean or overfitting. Open problem; DiverseGRPO-style diversity-aware optimization and "anchorless diversification" are early attempts.
2. **Reliable creative critique** — reward models that predict *taste and effectiveness* rather than generic "pleasingness," and that resist reward hacking. LitBench-style purpose-trained verifiers are the state of the art and still cap at ~78%.
3. **Grounding creative structure in measured performance** — closing the loop from generated creative → predicted behavioral outcome → schema update. The academic CTR-prediction line and the industry creative-DNA tagging line have not been unified into a generative-and-predictive loop. **This is the single biggest open opportunity and precisely the NeuroGraph × Atelier thesis.**
4. **Formal cinematic-language grammars for machines** — current systems handle continuity via memory graphs and shot-type heuristics, not principled cinematographic grammars; genuine room to innovate.
5. **Diversity-preserving multi-pass revision** — critique loops that improve quality without regressing to the mean.

**Commoditized / don't rebuild:**
- Text-to-image/video rendering and character consistency (StoryDiffusion, LTX, Runway, Flux + LoRA).
- Generic aesthetic reward models (LAION/ImageReward/HPS/PickScore — use off the shelf).
- Script→scene→shot decomposition scaffolding (well-documented multi-agent recipes; reimplement, don't research).
- Storyboard-as-intermediate-representation workflow (LTX has productized it).

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 — Build the schema layer first (weeks, not months).** Encode a symbolic "creative schema" that is your elaborated "template": archetype/narrative-arc taxonomy (Polti/Booker + right-brain feature set from System1), a hook taxonomy, a beat sheet, and — critically — brand-constraint and persona/decision-factor fields sourced from NeuroGraph. Represent it as structured JSON (like the storyboard-agent schemas in the multi-agent papers: what/where/who/props + shot parameters + hook type + emotional beat). This is your durable IP and the thing generation tools lack. *Benchmark to proceed: schema can round-trip a human art director's brief without losing strategic intent.*

**Stage 2 — Adopt the validated plan→draft→critique→revise harness, per-dimension controllable.** Directly reuse the DOC/Re3/Camera-Artist pattern: Director agent (schema → structured scene/shot plan) → Storyboarder agent (shots + cinematic language) → Critic agent (filter/rank) → Reviser. Expose *per-dimension* control strength (tight on brand/strategy, loose on imagery/phrasing) rather than one creativity slider — this is how you get DOC's coherence without its repetitiveness. *Benchmark: beat flat-prompt baseline on blind pairwise human preference by a margin comparable to DOC's ~20% interestingness gain.*

**Stage 3 — Encode taste as a curated dataset, not a prompt or a heavy fine-tune.** Build a continuously-curated house-style exemplar corpus per brand/vertical; retrieve exemplars into context; add lightweight per-brand LoRA/style vectors only once the corpus is stable (≥100+ exemplars). Explicitly sample diverse exemplars to fight homogenization. Avoid aggressive preference-tuning toward any single aesthetic reward. *Benchmark: on-brand human accept rate; watch a diversity metric (semantic-distance spread) and halt style-tuning if it drops.*

**Stage 4 — Build a performance-grounded critic (your moat).** Train a purpose-built reward/critic model on your domain, labeled where possible with NeuroGraph's performance signal (CTR/conversion/attention proxies) rather than generic aesthetics or self-critique. Use it as a ranker over multiple candidates, not as the sole definition of quality, and keep a human go/no-go gate. This is where you unify the two unconnected literatures (CTR prediction + creative DNA) into a generate→predict→select loop. Note the strategic tailwind: because marketers underestimate creative's contribution by ~2.5× (19% perceived vs. 49% actual per NCSolutions/Advertiser Perceptions), a tool that provably optimizes creative-driven lift is selling into a systematically undervalued category. *Benchmark: critic ranking correlates with held-out real performance better than an off-the-shelf aesthetic model; target >73% agreement with human preference (LitBench off-the-shelf ceiling) and rising toward 78%+.*

**Stage 5 — Evaluate like the field's best practice.** Pairwise human preference on a fixed rubric (coherence, on-brand, on-strategy, surprise/distinctiveness, predicted-performance) for ground truth; your trained critic as scalable proxy; never ship on BLEU/ROUGE or a raw LLM judge. Close the loop by A/B testing generated creative and feeding real CTR/conversion/attention back into the critic and schema.

**Thresholds that change the plan:** If small-sample style-LoRA consistently overfits (training-copy artifacts) → fall back to retrieval-only ICL. If your performance-grounded critic can't beat an off-the-shelf aesthetic model on held-out performance → the moat isn't real yet; invest more in NeuroGraph label quality before scaling generation. If diversity metrics collapse under any tuning → stop tuning and rely on structured diverse sampling.

## Caveats
- **Dates and recency:** the storyboard/video multi-agent literature is extremely fresh (many arXiv preprints dated 2025–2026) and largely un-peer-reviewed; treat specific system claims as directional, not settled. The foundational story-generation work (Plan-and-Write 2019, Re3 2022, DOC 2023, Dramatron CHI 2023) is solid and peer-reviewed.
- **Creative-effectiveness statistics** mix strong primary sources (Azimi WWW 2012; NCSolutions Aug 2023; Binet & Field IPA 2013) with credible-but-commissioned industry research (Dentsu, VidMob, System1, Nelson-Field/Amplified, Google/Meta self-reported). The often-quoted "creative = 50–70% of performance" range traces to the more defensible ~49–56% primary figures; the "2.5 seconds / 3 days per second" attention numbers are from conference presentations and a book, not an open peer-reviewed paper.
- **Evaluation is genuinely unsolved for creative quality** — even human experts show only moderate agreement (Fleiss κ 0.41 on TTCW), so "matching a particular taste" is inherently a moving, subjective target; your evaluation must be anchored to *your* curator's/brand's preferences and to downstream performance, not to any universal quality metric.
- **The homogenization and reward-hacking findings are robust and recent** — they are the strongest reason to be cautious about heavy preference-tuning and self-critique loops in a taste-driven product.
- Some specifics (Storyboard20K construction details, full TTCW methodology beyond the reported figures, and a few attention-research primary sources) lean partly on secondary summaries and are flagged accordingly; the core figures cited above were verified against primary or enricher-confirmed sources.
